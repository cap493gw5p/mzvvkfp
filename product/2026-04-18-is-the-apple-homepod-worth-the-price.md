# Is the Apple HomePod Worth the Price?

Our product picks are editor-tested, expert-approved. We may earn a commission through links on our site. Why Trust Us?

There’s no shortage of connected home speakers flooding the market. Apple’s the latest to toss their hat into the ring with the much-awaited HomePod. Designed to take on the likes of Amazon’s Echo and Google’s Home and Home Max in terms of digital assistance and functionality—thanks to the inclusion of Siri—but also compete with the Sonos One on the audio quality. But how does it actually perform on either of those fronts? Apple let us test a unit. Here’s what we thought.

Design

It’s no shocker that Apple churned out a handsome unit. The HomePod is cylindrical, and about 6.8-inches tall and wrapped in mesh. It bears a strong resemblance to Ultimate Ears’ Wonderboom Speaker at first blush, minus the enormous buttons, but bigger and more solid. The soft mesh coating belies its heft: a respectable 5.5 pounds, and you can opt for the HomePod in space grey or in white. Up top, there’s a flat bit where the graphical representation of Siri colorfully swirls when active, visual confirmation that the unit is active, and there are hidden touch controls below the panel: tapping or holding + or - for volume, using Siri, playing and pausing music and skipping tracks in either direction. It fits nicely in any corner of any room, and doesn’t take up much real estate on an coffee table or shelf.

Connectivity

The Apple HomePod is very much only for Apple users. To connect, you’ll need to have an iPhone 5S or later, iPad Air, iPad Pro, or anything else that’s running iOS 11.2.5 or later. The Homepod will tap into your Wi-Fi network to get online and then you’re only able to connect to it via Apple’s HomeKit. You can’t connect to it like a regular Bluetooth speaker, so if your buddy with an Android device is over and wants to play something from his phone, that’s not a possibility. That’s a serious bummer. You’ll need a $10-per-month Apple Music subscription if you want to use a streaming music service to be controlled by your voice. Apple doesn’t allow Pandora or Spotify to directly connect (as they can with Echo, Sonos or Google Home), though you can use access those services directly on your device and stream to the HomePod. It does support AirPlay if you want to use it with your Apple TV or computer, though. It’s just annoying that you have to have your device nearby to use free services, but perhaps you’ll make that sacrifice for the sound quality.

Audio Components

Packed into the HomePod you’ve got one 4-inch central woofer, facing upward, booming from the center of the unit, 7 tweeters encircling the outside to ensure 360-degree sound. Apple’s also stuffed room-sensing technology in there, meaning no matter where you place the speaker, the audio will adapt to the location to maximize the sound quality. And you’ve got 7 microphones, in an array, so it can hear you from afar. None of this is really groundbreaking, as most of the competition boast similar setups. It’s all powered by an A8 chip, which is universal across many an Apple device.

Sound Quality

The HomePod sounds really good. Really, really good. It can indeed fill a room with booming bass and clear highs. My favorite rap albums were so bass-heavy, the table the HomePod was on was vibrating. There’s a little distortion at full volume, but it’s not that bad. If you’re an audiophile, you’re probably going to have a far better system and speakers to enjoy music from, so for the rest of us, with simple everyday use cases, it’s great. When stacked against the Echo, the HomePod wins the sound contest handily. The Google Home also proves no match. When it comes to the Sonos One, however, the sound quality difference is negligible. Closing your eyes and try to tell which device is playing is a trickier task than not.

Digital Assistance

Siri in the HomePod is most effective when it comes to playing music. Ask for a workout playlist or a calming meditation mix and you’ll get it (again provided you have the access to Apple Music, otherwise it’s just culling from your limited purchased music database). You can even ask Siri for trivial factoids about whatever song is playing, like when it was recorded or whether the artist won a Grammy. Cute, but not terribly useful.

Siri also botched or misunderstood more than half my commands, even when I was standing right on top of the unit. “Play some fun music,” resulted in hearing the band Fun’s “Some Nights.” Not great, but maybe later software updates will see that improving.

When it comes to everything else, Siri is perhaps less potent than you’re used to than when in your phone. It can control anything your HomeKit can connect to, so you’ve got options like Hue Lighting or Honeywell’s thermostats (check out the full list of HomeKit connectivity partners here), but if you’re a Nest owner—a Google product—you’re out of luck here.

Siri can also do stuff like send messages, or set timers, read you the weather, get you traffic or travel times, read headlines or check sports scores. It can be used a speaker for your iPhone calls, but you can’t ask it to call anyone directly. You can set a timer, but only one (Google Home allows multiple timers). As of now, you can only connect one user’s phone to the unit. I can only access my music selection, an annoyance for my fiancee. That also means any personal data used is being culled from my AppleID. That’s less than stellar. Downfield, here’s hoping Apple allows multiple users for the HomePod.

Multiroom and Stereo Speaker Pairing

Currently, you cannot pair two HomePods, like you can with Sonos, meaning no stereo HomePod sound—yet. Future versions of AirPlay and new iOS 11 functionality promises that you will be able to, via a free software update, but at this point, if you want to start your song in the kitchen and finish it upstairs in a bedroom, you’re not able to.

Price

While it may sound the best, you’ll pay for it. The Amazon Echo clocks in at $85, while the Google Home is $129 and the Sonos One is $200. For $150 more, you can get two Amazon Sonos Ones, bundled together. And for $50 less, you’ll get the Apple HomePod, retailing for $349.

Worth It or Not?

If you’re anything less than an Apple lover, as it currently stands, this may not be the smart speaker for you. If you’re an Apple addict, living entirely within the Apple ecosystem, it’s definitely worth it. Any glitches will (hopefully) be soon addressed and corrected, but the sound is great, the design is modern and fresh, and it’s a perfect addition to your home. You wouldn’t have regrets.
