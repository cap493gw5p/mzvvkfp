Belly fat during menopause is common and usually results from a combination of factors: declining estrogen levels, a slower metabolism, loss of muscle mass, and lifestyle influences such as diet, stress, and reduced physical activity. Hormonal shifts cause fat to redistribute toward the abdomen, even in women who haven't gained overall weight.

Because belly fat can also signal underlying health concerns like insulin resistance or thyroid imbalance, understanding the root cause matters. Explanation

During menopause, women experience changes in their bodies that can lead to gaining belly fat. Hormonal Changes: As women go through menopause, their levels of estrogen decrease. This hormonal change can cause fat to be stored more in the belly area instead of other parts of the body. Metabolism: Metabolism tends to slow down with age, making it easier to gain weight. Lifestyle Factors: Diet and physical activity are important too. Eating more calories than the body uses or not getting enough exercise can contribute to weight gain.
