DATING IS HARD, period. But when you’re polyamorous, meaning you’re open to having romantic relationships with more than one person at a time, it can feel even harder. Our society tends to view monogamy as the default relationship design, so if you’re into polyamory or other forms of non-monogamy, it’s easy to feel like you’re the only one out there.

That's especially true when you're looking for partners on more mainstream dating apps, says polyamory educator Leanne Yau—which is why getting on a polyamorous dating app can be so beneficial. “I often had to explain my [polyamorous] situation to people in detail only for them to reject or judge me,” she says. “People would tell me I shouldn’t be on dating apps if I already had a partner, or that my relationship was a joke and a failure. Or they'd slut-shame my partner or me and then get mad at me for their singlehood.” She’s also had people misunderstand her dynamic and desires, equating polyamory with non-commitment. (Yau remembers people telling her, “You're polyamorous? Cool, I want something casual too!”)

The growing interest in non-monogamy has been accompanied by a growing number of apps that specifically cater to polyamorous people. We’ve also seen some older apps, like OkCupid, update their user interface to make it easier for polyamorous people to connect with like-minded partners.

Every app on this list was either designed specifically for non-monogamous folks, or it’s a mainstream dating app that has some way to filter searches for other poly people, whether it's through pre-set filters, tags, or an “about me” section where you can describe your relationship style. Some of these poly dating apps even let you link your account with a partner, so you can search for matches as a unit. (Alas, you still can’t link your profile with multiple partners in any of these apps; if you’re an app developer, that’s a million-dollar idea right there.)

Here are the 9 best relationship apps for polyamorous folks.

Feeld

“If you’re non-monogamous, you know Feeld,” says Gabrielle Smith, Non-Monogamy Educator and Board Member of the Organization for Polyamory and Ethical Non-Monogamy.

Feeld is excellent because it’s for all alternative relationship styles and very inclusive of kink and various desires, Smith says. With Feeld, you can also create group chats with prospective singles, couples, or even more folks. Additionally, Feeld is one of those apps where you can link your profile with a partner’s.

#open

The name of this app makes it pretty clear that it’s for non-monogamous folks. #open uses hashtags to help you find and connect with people with similar desires, kinks, and interests. Using hashtags is “a unique way for people to connect over things you care about in a relationship,” Yau says. “It’s also an app made by non-monogamous people for non-monogamous people.”

Download on iOS and Google Play.

OkCupid

OkCupid has been around for quite some time, but they were actually one of the first apps to update and offer non-monogamy options and filters, Smith notes. It also allows you to link with a partner. With its many, many questions it asks users, “You can get as deep or as shallow as you want, and the questionnaire that determines compatibility is GREAT for weeding out folks who are walking red flags,” Smith says.

Download the app on iOS and Google Play.

Hinge

Hinge has a relationship goal section where you can state if you’re looking for long-term relationships, short-term ones, or both. “More recently, it’s introduced monogamous and non-monogamous as a filter and allows you to explain more about the exact type of (non-monogamous) relationship you want,” Yau says. So you can get really specific about your desires and the type of polyamorous relationship you’re seeking.

“There’s also a limited number of people you can match to and talk to in a day, so people are pickier on Hinge, and therefore take more time to seriously consider each profile, as opposed to [engaging in] trigger happy swiping.” So even though Hinge’s tagline is “the app that’s designed to be deleted,” it’s actually becoming quite polyamory-friendly.
