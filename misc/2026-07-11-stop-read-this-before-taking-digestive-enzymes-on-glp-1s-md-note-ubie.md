Doctor's Note: Should You Take Digestive Enzymes on GLP-1s? A Doctor Weighs In

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Digestive enzyme supplements may help relieve common GLP-1 side effects such as bloating, fullness, and fatty stools. However, large-scale clinical trials in GLP-1 users are limited, and most supporting evidence is anecdotal.

Start with a broad-spectrum enzyme product at a low dose taken with meals

Combine supplementation with dietary and lifestyle adjustments

Monitor your response and consult a healthcare provider if symptoms persist

Explanation

Many people taking GLP-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1s) for diabetes or weight management wonder if adding digestive enzymes can help ease digestive side effects and boost nutrient absorption. Below, we'll explore what the research says about GLP-1 digestive enzymes benefits, common digestive issues with GLP-1s, and practical tips to decide if enzyme supplements make sense for you.

What Are GLP-1 Receptor Agonists?

GLP-1s (glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists) are drugs such as semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy) and liraglutide (Victoza, Saxenda) that mimic a natural gut hormone. They work by:

Slowing gastric emptying—food stays in your stomach longer

Stimulating insulin release when blood sugar is high

These effects help lower blood sugar and support weight loss but can also change how you digest and absorb nutrients.

What Are Digestive Enzymes?

Digestive enzymes are proteins your body produces (in saliva, stomach, pancreas) that break down carbohydrates, proteins, and fats into absorbable forms. Common supplemental enzymes include:

Lipase: breaks down fats

Protease: digests proteins

Amylase: converts starches into sugars

Lactase: splits lactose in dairy

Over-the-counter enzyme blends may also include cellulase, alpha-galactosidase, or other plant-based enzymes.

Potential GLP-1 Digestive Enzymes Benefits

Adding digestive enzymes while on GLP-1 therapy might:

Improve nutrient absorption if slowed gastric emptying reduces enzyme–food contact

Reduce bloating by breaking down gas-producing carbohydrates

Ease mild diarrhea by supporting fat digestion

Enhance overall comfort, leading to better medication adherence

However, high-quality clinical trials on enzyme supplements in GLP-1 users are limited. Most evidence is anecdotal or extrapolated from studies in pancreatic insufficiency or general digestive disorders.

What the Research Says

Gastric Emptying and Enzyme ActivityGLP-1s slow gastric emptying, which theoretically could reduce the mixing of natural digestive enzymes with food. Small studies in other contexts suggest enzyme supplements may compensate when digestive secretions are delayed.

Fat MalabsorptionSome GLP-1 users report greasy stools or steatorrhea. In pancreatic enzyme deficiency, lipase supplementation consistently improves fat digestion. Whether similar benefits extend to GLP-1–induced delay remains unproven but plausible.

Carbohydrate and Protein DigestionGLP-1 therapy doesn't block carbohydrate or protein breakdown directly. Still, if food sits longer in the stomach, supplemental amylase or protease could help reduce bloating or discomfort for some.

Bottom line: While enzyme supplements show promise for general digestive support, no large-scale trials have specifically tested their use in GLP-1 patients. More research is needed.

Who Might Benefit Most?

Consider a trial of digestive enzymes if you have:

Persistent bloating, fullness, or gas

Recurrent loose, fatty stools

Mild nausea linked to large or fatty meals

No other treatable cause identified by your healthcare provider

Enzyme supplements are generally safe, with few side effects beyond mild abdominal cramping or allergic reactions in sensitive individuals.

How to Choose and Use Enzyme Supplements

Select a Broad-Spectrum BlendLook for products containing lipase, protease, and amylase. Some formulas add lactase or cellulase for dairy and fiber digestion.

Start with the Lowest Effective DoseBegin at half the recommended dose with your largest meal. Increase gradually if needed, up to the full label dose.

Take with MealsEnzymes work best when chewed or swallowed at the start of a meal, so they mix with incoming food.

Monitor Your SymptomsKeep a simple diary of meals, enzyme dose, and digestive responses. This helps you and your doctor fine-tune your regimen.

Lifestyle and Dietary Tips

Enzyme supplements are only one piece of the puzzle. You can also:

Eat smaller, more frequent meals to reduce gastric load

Limit high-fat, greasy foods that linger in the stomach

Stay hydrated and include fiber gradually to prevent constipation

Chew thoroughly to kick-start natural salivary enzymes

Experiment with probiotics or fermented foods (yogurt, kefir) to support healthy gut flora

When to Seek Medical Advice

Most mild digestive complaints improve over time or with simple adjustments. But speak to a healthcare professional if you experience:

Severe abdominal pain

Blood in stool or persistent vomiting

Unintentional weight loss beyond your treatment goals

Signs of dehydration (dizziness, dark urine)
