How can you differentiate between myasthenia gravis and Lambert-Eaton syndrome?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Myasthenia gravis (MG) and Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome (LEMS) are both autoimmune neuromuscular disorders, but they differ in cause, symptoms, and progression.

Key differences:

Myasthenia gravis: Typically begins with weakness in the eyes and face (drooping eyelids, double vision, trouble swallowing). Worsens with activity.

Lambert-Eaton syndrome: Usually starts with weakness in the legs and hips, making it hard to walk or climb stairs. Often improves briefly with activity and is frequently linked to small-cell lung cancer.

Because these conditions share overlapping symptoms with many other neurological and muscular disorders, early evaluation is critical. Explanation

Myasthenia gravis and Lambert-Eaton syndrome are both conditions that cause muscle weakness, but they have some important differences.

Causes and Symptoms:

Myasthenia Gravis (MG): This condition is caused by a problem at the connection between nerves and muscles. It often starts with symptoms like droopy eyelids or double vision because it affects the muscles around the eyes and face first.

[Know Now Doctor](https://cellular.wiki/uorep5h/)

Lambert-Eaton Syndrome (LES): This syndrome is also a problem with nerve-muscle communication, but it often begins with weakness in the legs. People with LES might find it hard to get up from a chair or climb stairs.

Diagnosis:

Electrophysiological Testing: In MG, repetitive nerve stimulation tests may show a decrease in muscle response. In LES, the muscle response might improve after exercise or repeated stimulation.

Understanding these differences helps doctors decide on the best treatment for each condition.
