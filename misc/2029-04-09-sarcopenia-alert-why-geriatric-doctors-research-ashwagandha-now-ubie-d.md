What Is Sarcopenia?

Sarcopenia isn't just "getting weaker." It's a medical condition with far-reaching impacts:

Progressive loss of skeletal muscle mass and function

Reduced mobility, balance issues and higher fall risk

Lower quality of life and increased healthcare needs

Major drivers include hormonal changes (lower testosterone, growth hormone), chronic inflammation and reduced physical activity. Traditional approaches focus on resistance exercise and adequate protein intake—but doctors are seeking additional, safe tools.

What Is Ashwagandha?

Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) is an herb used for centuries in Ayurvedic medicine. It's classified as an adaptogen—meaning it may help the body cope with stress. Key active compounds (withanolides) have been studied for:

Anti-inflammatory effects

Antioxidant activity

Potential hormone-balancing benefits

These properties suggest a role in supporting muscle health, especially for older adults facing hormonal shifts and chronic low-grade inflammation.

Why Geriatric Doctors Are Interested

Muscle Mass and Strength Gains • A 2015 trial in healthy men (age 18–50) found that 300–600 mg/day of ashwagandha extract for eight weeks boosted muscle size and strength significantly more than placebo. • Early pilot studies in older adults suggest similar trends—a modest increase in hand-grip strength and stair-climb performance over 12 weeks.

Inflammation and Recovery • Chronic inflammation accelerates muscle breakdown. Ashwagandha's withanolides inhibit inflammatory pathways (e.g., NF-κB), potentially reducing muscle damage. • Reduced muscle soreness may help elderly individuals stick with resistance training longer.

Hormonal Support • Some studies report small increases in testosterone and thyroid-stimulating hormone. While absolute changes are modest, even slight hormonal boosts can help preserve lean mass in seniors.

Stress Reduction • High cortisol (the "stress hormone") promotes muscle protein breakdown. Ashwagandha has been shown to lower cortisol levels by up to 30% in non-elderly adults, possibly helping protect muscle tissue.

Safety and Side Effects

Ashwagandha is generally well tolerated, but geriatric doctors consider:

Gastrointestinal discomfort (nausea, diarrhea) in up to 10% of users

Possible interactions with thyroid medications, sedatives or immunosuppressants

Rare allergic reactions

Typical doses in studies range from 300 to 600 mg of a standardized root extract, taken once or twice daily. Starting at the lower end (300 mg/day) and monitoring response is a common clinical strategy.

Balanced Nutrition • Include antioxidants (fruits, vegetables), healthy fats (olive oil, nuts) and whole grains.
