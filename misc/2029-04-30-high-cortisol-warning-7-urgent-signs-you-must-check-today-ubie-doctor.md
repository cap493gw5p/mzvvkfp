How do I know if my cortisol is too high?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Elevated cortisol levels may indicate Cushing's syndrome, a hormonal disorder caused by prolonged exposure to high cortisol. Common diagnostic tests include the late-night salivary cortisol test, 24-hour urinary free cortisol test, and low-dose dexamethasone suppression test. Symptoms can include weight gain, a rounded face, high blood pressure, muscle weakness, and skin changes. If you suspect your cortisol is high, consult a healthcare provider for accurate testing and diagnosis.

Explanation

Cortisol is a hormone that helps your body handle stress, but too much can lead to health problems like Cushing's syndrome. Signs of high cortisol can include weight gain, especially around the belly, high blood pressure, and feeling tired. If you're experiencing these symptoms, you can start by using Ubie's free AI symptom checker to better understand what might be causing them.

Testing for High Cortisol:

Late-night Salivary Cortisol Test: This simple test measures cortisol levels in your saliva at night when they should be low. If levels are high, it could mean there's too much cortisol in your body.

Other Tests: Doctors may use other tests like blood or urine tests to check cortisol levels.
