# ​How This Dad Inspired His 10-Year-Old Daughter to Become a Competitive CrossFitter

When Bob Portell is grinding through the final, gut-wrenching rep of his evening CrossFit WOD, the first person he thinks of is his daughter, Josie.

“When I’m in that deep dark place, I think about how hard she works and pushes in her life and I say, ‘Hey, I can definitely push through this because she pushes so hard every single day,’” says Portell.

Josie was born with spina bifida—a birth defect that prevents proper development of the spinal cord—and has been in a wheelchair since she was 2 years old. But that hasn’t stopped her from participating in everything from track, basketball, and most recently, CrossFit.

“One day, Josie saw me doing CrossFit and she said, ‘Dad, I want to do that!’ So I started doing research on adaptive programming and all that sort of stuff,” says Bob.

Josie is naturally very passionate about fitness, but seeing the challenges of CrossFit and the community aspect is what drove her to want to try, he adds. Knowing that the strength and conditioning workouts would help Josie excel in her favorite sports—basketball and track—helped sweeten the pot, too. Bob quickly learned one of the most effective ways to get kids moving is to explain how it can help them do the things they already love doing.

Since then, Josie has been doing CrossFit for 9 months and competed at Wodapalooza this spring, a worldwide fitness festival that invites only the most elite athletes from 30 different countries to compete in a series of CrossFit WODs for 4 days. Bob is now CrossFit Level 1 certified and plans to bring a kids program to their home gym in St Louis, MO to get kids more involved and excited about fitness.

“CrossFit has been great for me and great for her because we have this whole connection when we do the partner WODs. It’s our thing now. We partner up and we push each other—most of the time I’m the only slowing her down!” says Bob.

Bob and Josie aren’t the only pair who’ve found strength in numbers. A Michigan State University study found that hitting the gym with a buddy can double the length of your workout. What’s more, a UK study found people who exercised in groups as opposed to solo sessions burned an average of 41 more calories per workout.

For Bob, he didn’t have to force Josie to get active or competitive. But for dads who aren’t so lucky, he suggests finding ways to make fitness fun while striking a delicate balance.

“If you make it seem like a chore, it could tarnish them and make them never want to do it again,” says Bob. “They’ll remember that activity sucking as a kid and not want to do it. It’s a delicate balance. You don’t want to push, but you do want to encourage.”

Josie’s performance and persistence in the gym is the foundation of the Portell family.

“When she comes to obstacles in her regular life, she knows she can push through it because of how she has pushed through her workouts,” says Bob. “When she wants to do something, we figure out a way. We never let the wheelchair stop her. We always find a way.”
