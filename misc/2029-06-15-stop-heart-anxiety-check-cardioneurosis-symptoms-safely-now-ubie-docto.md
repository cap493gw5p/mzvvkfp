Explanation

Cardioneurosis is a condition where anxiety and stress trigger heart-related sensations—palpitations, chest tightness, shortness of breath—even though there's no underlying heart disease. For many, the cycle of fear about heart health worsens symptoms, creating real distress. If you experience exhaustion and chest tightness when anxious, this guide will help you understand cardioneurosis and perform a safe self-check before seeing a professional.

What Is Cardioneurosis?

Also called "neurocirculatory asthenia" or "Da Costa's syndrome."

Involves heightened sensitivity of the heart to stress and anxiety.

Palpitations (rapid or irregular heartbeat)

Chest tightness or pressure

Breathlessness

Excessive fatigue

While the sensations feel alarming, cardioneurosis is linked to nervous-system reactions rather than structural heart disease.

Recognizing Key Symptoms

Understanding your symptoms helps you separate anxiety-related sensations from true emergencies. Common signs include:

Exhaustion and chest tightness when anxiousStress can trigger muscle tension around your ribs and diaphragm, leading to both fatigue and a heavy, constricted feeling in the chest.

Heart palpitations or fluttering

Shallow, rapid breathing or feeling unable to take a full breath

Lightheadedness or dizziness

Sweating, trembling, or a feeling of impending doom

If these occur primarily in moments of high stress, they often point to a nervous-system origin.

Why Safe Symptom Checking Matters

Self-assessment is useful for monitoring patterns and triggers, but it's not a substitute for professional evaluation. Checking yourself safely helps you:

Identify true emergencies versus anxiety reactions

Track symptom frequency, duration, and severity

Prepare accurate information for your healthcare provider

Reduce unnecessary ER visits or tests

Always err on the side of caution: if you're ever in doubt about chest pain or severe breathlessness, treat it as an emergency.

Self-Assessment Guide

Follow these steps to check your symptoms thoughtfully and reduce panic:

Pause and Breathe

Sit upright in a calm environment.

Inhale slowly through your nose for 4 seconds, hold for 2 seconds, exhale through your mouth for 6 seconds.

Repeat until your heart rate slows and breathing steadies.

Note Your Experience

When did symptoms start?

What were you doing? (e.g., work stress, arguments, physical exertion)

Rate intensity from 1 (mild) to 10 (severe).

Check Your Pulse

Find your pulse at the wrist or neck.

Count beats for 30 seconds and multiply by 2 for beats per minute.

A normal resting rate is 60–100 bpm; higher rates during stress are common.

Observe Other Signs

Skin: Is it sweaty, pale, or flushed?

Breathing: Is it shallow or rapid?

Mind: Are you experiencing fear of losing control or fainting?

Keep a Symptom Diary

Use a journal or an app.

Record triggers (e.g., caffeine, late nights), symptoms, and coping steps you took.

Red Flags: When to Seek Immediate Help

Some symptoms require urgent medical attention. Call emergency services or head to the nearest ER if you experience:

Loss of consciousness or near-fainting

Severe shortness of breath at rest

Profuse sweating with nausea or vomiting

Confusion, slurred speech, or weakness on one side of the body

These signs may indicate a heart attack or another serious condition.

Tips for Managing Anxiety-Related Heart Symptoms

Limit stimulants (caffeine, nicotine) that can worsen palpitations

Maintain regular sleep, diet, and moderate exercise

Practice daily stress reduction (deep breathing, guided imagery)

Build a support network—talk to friends, family, or support groups

Consider cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT) for long-term anxiety relief

Speak to a Doctor

Only a qualified healthcare professional can diagnose or rule out serious conditions. If your symptoms ever feel life-threatening, do not hesitate—seek immediate medical attention. For ongoing concerns, schedule a thorough evaluation and share your symptom diary for the most accurate care plan.

By understanding cardioneurosis and how to check your symptoms safely, you can reduce fear and take proactive steps toward better health. Always prioritize professional guidance for anything serious or life-threatening.
