What are common sleep disorders in children and how are they treated?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Common sleep disorders in children include insomnia (difficulty falling or staying asleep), obstructive sleep apnea (breathing interruptions during sleep), and night terrors (episodes of intense fear during deep sleep). Treatment typically starts with behavioral changes—such as consistent bedtime routines, limiting screen time, and improving sleep hygiene—and may include medical interventions like CPAP therapy or medication when needed.

If your child is struggling with sleep, identifying the underlying cause early can make a meaningful difference in their health, mood, and development. Because symptoms of sleep disorders often overlap with other conditions, guessing can delay proper care.
