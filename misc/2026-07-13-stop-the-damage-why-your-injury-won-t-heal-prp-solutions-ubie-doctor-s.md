Why Injuries Sometimes Don't Heal

Injury healing is a complex process involving inflammation, tissue repair, and remodeling. When it stalls, consider these common factors:

Blood flow issues• Reduced circulation slows delivery of oxygen and nutrients.• Smoking, diabetes or vascular disease can impair blood flow.

Inadequate rest or overuse• Continual stress on injured tissue prevents repair.• Returning to activity too soon can restart inflammation.

Poor nutrition• Lack of protein, vitamins C and D, zinc and other nutrients delays collagen production.• Dehydration makes tissues less elastic.

Underlying medical conditions• Autoimmune diseases (e.g., rheumatoid arthritis) can disrupt healing.• Hormonal imbalances (e.g., thyroid disorders) affect cell regeneration.

Infection or chronic inflammation• Unrecognized infection creates a hostile environment for healing.• Chronic inflammation can lead to scar tissue rather than healthy repair.

If you suspect an ongoing infection, worsening pain, or signs of poor circulation—such as numbness or color changes—please speak to a doctor immediately or use this free AI symptom checker to quickly assess what might be causing your symptoms and determine whether you need immediate medical attention.

Who's a Good Candidate for PRP?

PRP is not a one-size-fits-all solution. Ideal candidates typically:

Have chronic injuries (3+ months) that haven't fully responded to rest, physical therapy, or medications

Wish to avoid long-term use of painkillers or steroids

Are in generally good health without active infection or severe blood disorders

Understand that multiple sessions (often 1–3) may be needed for optimal results

People with platelet disorders, uncontrolled diabetes, or active cancer may not be suitable candidates. Always discuss your full medical history with a qualified provider.

Medical Next Steps: Beyond PRP

While PRP can be a powerful tool, comprehensive care often involves a multifaceted approach:

Mechanical stabilization• Bracing or taping to off-load stress on injured tissues• Custom orthotics for foot and ankle injuries

Physical therapy• Progressive strengthening and flexibility exercises• Manual therapy to improve joint mobility and tissue quality

Nutrition optimization• Adequate protein, omega-3 fats, vitamins D and C, zinc and magnesium• Hydration strategies before and after workouts

Pain and inflammation management• Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) for short-term relief• Cryotherapy (ice) and contrast baths

Lifestyle modifications• Smoking cessation to improve circulation• Sleep hygiene—7–9 hours per night for tissue repair

Advanced imaging and diagnostics• Ultrasound or MRI to re-evaluate the extent of injury• Lab tests to rule out anemia, diabetes or autoimmune conditions

Setting Realistic Expectations

PRP can accelerate healing, but it's not a magic bullet. Manage expectations by:

Understanding that results vary—some patients feel improvement in 4–6 weeks, others need more time.

Planning for a multi-disciplinary approach: PRP plus rehab, nutrition, and lifestyle changes.

Anticipating possible soreness at the injection site for 2–3 days post-procedure.

Potential Side Effects and Safety

Since PRP uses your own blood, serious complications are rare. Mild risks include:

Temporary pain or swelling at the injection site

Minor bruising from the blood draw

Infection (very uncommon when performed under sterile conditions)

Discuss any allergies, medications (like blood thinners), or bleeding disorders with your provider before proceeding.

When to Seek Further Medical Attention

While most injuries improve with conservative care and PRP, certain warning signs require immediate medical attention:

Sudden increase in pain, redness or swelling around the injury

Fever, chills or other signs of systemic infection

Loss of mobility, numbness or weakness in the limb

Uncontrolled bleeding or significant bruising

If you experience any of these symptoms—or if you're simply unsure about your condition—speak to a doctor right away. You can also try this AI-powered symptom checker to get personalized insights about your symptoms and understand whether you need urgent care.

Talking to Your Doctor

Before moving forward with PRP for sports injuries, have a detailed conversation with your healthcare provider:

Review your complete medical history, including medications and supplements

Discuss the expected benefits, timelines and costs (PRP is often not covered by insurance)

Plan a personalized rehabilitation program to complement PRP

Clarify follow-up schedules and any imaging needed
