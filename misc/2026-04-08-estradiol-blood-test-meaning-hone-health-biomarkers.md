What is Estradiol?

[Blood Test](https://calmvector.dns0663.com/blog/2334638643.html)

Estradiol is a hormone that influences multiple organ systems, including the reproductive, cardiovascular, skeletal, and central nervous system.

In women, estradiol is produced by the ovaries. It regulates ovulation, supports the uterine lining, and drives the menstrual cycle. As women enter perimenopause, estradiol levels fluctuate widely, which can lead to symptoms including irregular or absent menstrual cycles, hot flashes, sleep disruption, vaginal dryness, and reduced bone density.

How to Interpret Estradiol

In women, estradiol fluctuates throughout the menstrual cycle, and a single blood test only captures your level on the day it was taken. In women with regular cycles, estradiol is relatively low at the start of the cycle, rises as ovarian follicles develop, peaks just before ovulation, then briefly declines before rising again during the luteal phase.

For this reason, physicians interpret estradiol levels in the context of where you are in your cycle. If cycles are irregular or cycle timing is unclear, clinicians evaluate estradiol alongside other hormones such as follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) and progesterone, as well as symptoms and cycle history.

*During perimenopause, estradiol levels may become more erratic as ovarian hormone production becomes less consistent
