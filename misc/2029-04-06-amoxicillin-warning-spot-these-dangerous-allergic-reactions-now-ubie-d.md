# Amoxicillin Warning: Spot These Dangerous Allergic Reactions Now | Ubie Doctor's Note

Published on: 4/13/2026

Taking Amoxicillin? Common Side Effects and Signs of an Allergic Reaction

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Amoxicillin side effects most often include nausea, diarrhea, mild rash, yeast infections, headache, and fatigue. These are typically temporary and resolve on their own.

Seek emergency care for allergic reaction signs: hives, wheezing, trouble breathing, or facial/throat swelling. Call your doctor promptly for severe or bloody diarrhea, yellowing of the skin or eyes, dark urine, or a widespread feverish rash.

Explanation

Amoxicillin is one of the most commonly prescribed antibiotics. Doctors use it to treat bacterial infections such as ear infections, sinus infections, strep throat, pneumonia, urinary tract infections, and certain skin infections. For most people, it is safe and effective.

However, like all medications, amoxicillin side effects can occur. Some are mild and temporary. Others may signal a more serious reaction that needs prompt medical care.

If you are currently taking amoxicillin—or about to start—here is what you need to know.

Common Amoxicillin Side Effects

Most amoxicillin side effects are mild and improve once your body adjusts to the medication or after the antibiotic course is finished.

1. Digestive Problems

Digestive issues are the most frequently reported amoxicillin side effects. These may include:

Nausea

Vomiting

Diarrhea

Stomach pain or cramping

Loss of appetite

These symptoms happen because antibiotics not only kill harmful bacteria, but can also disrupt the "good" bacteria in your gut.

What you can do:

Take amoxicillin exactly as prescribed.

Take it with food (if your doctor or pharmacist says it's okay).

Stay hydrated if you have diarrhea.

If diarrhea becomes severe, watery, bloody, or persistent, contact your doctor. In rare cases, antibiotics can cause a more serious intestinal infection.

2. Mild Skin Rash

A skin rash is another known amoxicillin side effect. Not all rashes are allergic reactions.

Some people—especially children—develop a flat, red rash several days into treatment. This type of rash:

Is usually not itchy

Appears on the chest, back, or abdomen

May spread gradually

Often resolves on its own

However, because rashes can sometimes signal an allergy, it's important not to ignore them.

If you notice any skin changes while taking amoxicillin, you can check your symptoms using a free online tool to help determine whether your symptoms need immediate medical attention.

3. Yeast Infections

Because amoxicillin changes normal bacteria levels, it may increase the risk of yeast infections.

Women may notice:

Vaginal itching

Thick white discharge

Some people may also develop oral thrush (white patches in the mouth).

These are uncomfortable but generally treatable. Speak to your healthcare provider if symptoms appear.

4. Headache or Fatigue

Less commonly, people report:

Headache

Mild fatigue

General feeling of being unwell

These symptoms are usually temporary. If they are severe or worsening, contact your doctor.

Signs of an Allergic Reaction to Amoxicillin

An allergic reaction is different from typical amoxicillin side effects. While most reactions are mild, some can become serious quickly.

About 1–10% of people report a penicillin allergy, though many who believe they are allergic are not truly allergic when formally tested.

Mild to Moderate Allergic Reaction

Symptoms may include:

Itchy rash

Hives (raised, red, itchy welts)

Swelling of the skin

Mild wheezing

These symptoms can appear within hours or even days after starting the medication.

If you notice hives or swelling, stop taking the medication and contact your doctor right away. You may need a different antibiotic.

Severe Allergic Reaction (Anaphylaxis)

A severe allergic reaction is rare but can be life-threatening. It usually happens quickly—often within minutes to hours of taking the medication.

Watch for:

Difficulty breathing

Swelling of the face, lips, tongue, or throat

Tightness in the throat

Rapid heartbeat

Dizziness or fainting

Delayed Allergic Reactions

Some allergic reactions happen days or even weeks after starting amoxicillin.

Widespread rash

Fever

Joint pain

Swollen lymph nodes

Though less dramatic than anaphylaxis, these reactions still require prompt medical evaluation.

Who Is More Likely to Experience Amoxicillin Side Effects?

Certain factors may increase your risk:

History of penicillin allergy

Asthma or other allergic conditions

Previous drug reactions

Kidney disease

Taking multiple medications

If you've ever had a reaction to penicillin or another antibiotic, tell your doctor before starting amoxicillin.

When to Call Your Doctor

Contact your healthcare provider if you experience:

A new rash

Persistent vomiting

Severe or bloody diarrhea

Yellowing of the skin or eyes

Unusual bruising or bleeding

Dark urine

Extreme fatigue

These could signal uncommon but serious amoxicillin side effects that need medical attention.

If something feels significantly wrong, trust your instincts and seek care.

When to Seek Emergency Care

Call emergency services immediately if you have:

Trouble breathing

Swelling of the face or throat

Fainting

Severe hives with other symptoms

These may be signs of anaphylaxis, which requires urgent treatment.

Can You Prevent Amoxicillin Side Effects?

You can't completely prevent side effects, but you can reduce risks:

Take the medication exactly as prescribed.

Finish the full course unless your doctor tells you to stop.

Do not share antibiotics with others.

Inform your provider of all medications and supplements you take.

Never stop amoxicillin early without medical guidance unless you suspect a serious allergic reaction.

What If You Think You're Allergic to Amoxicillin?

Many people labeled as "penicillin allergic" are not truly allergic. If your reaction was mild or happened years ago, your doctor may suggest allergy testing.

Confirming whether you truly have a penicillin allergy is important. Avoiding penicillin unnecessarily can lead to:

Use of broader antibiotics

Higher healthcare costs

Increased antibiotic resistance

Discuss this with your doctor if you're unsure.

The Bottom Line on Amoxicillin Side Effects

Amoxicillin is widely used and generally safe. Most amoxicillin side effects are mild and manageable, especially digestive symptoms or minor rashes.

However, allergic reactions—while uncommon—can be serious. Knowing the difference between common side effects and warning signs of an allergic reaction helps you act quickly if needed.

Remember:

Mild nausea or diarrhea is common.

A rash should always be evaluated.

Breathing problems or facial swelling require emergency care.

If you notice any unusual symptoms, speak to a doctor promptly. Anything that feels severe, rapidly worsening, or life-threatening should be treated as an emergency.

Your health and safety come first. When in doubt, get medical advice.
