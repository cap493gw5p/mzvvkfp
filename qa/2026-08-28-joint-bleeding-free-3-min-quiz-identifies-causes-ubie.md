About the Symptom

Bleeding inside joint can occur after an injury, even a minor one, or spontaneously. It is also called hemarthrosis or articular bleeding.

When to See a Doctor

Bleeding inside joint

Possible Causes

Generally, Joint bleeding can be related to:

Essential thrombocythemia is a rare disorder where the body produces too many platelets, which are blood cells that stick together to form clots. It may be caused by several acquired gene mutations, which are not inherited.

Congenital hemophilia is a genetic bleeding disorder resulting in insufficient blood clotting due to a deficiency of clotting factors.

Immune thrombocytopenic purpura (ITP) happens when the immune system mistakenly attacks and destroys platelets, which are blood cells that help with clotting. ITP can cause low platelet levels, leading to easy bruising and bleeding. Triggers include infections like HIV and hepatitis.

von Willebrand Disease (vWD)

Acquired Hemophilia

Amyloidosis

Liver Cirrhosis

Alcohol-related liver disease

Related Serious Diseases

Sometimes, Joint bleeding may be related to these serious diseases:

Leukemia is a cancer of the body's blood cell-forming tissues. Blood cells circulate in our bloodstream and are created and matured in the bone marrow and the lymphatic system. Leukemia involves white blood cells and lymphatic cells and has several types. The cancerous cells are produced in excess and do not function normally. Leukemias can be aggressive and cause symptoms or chronic and smolder for years.

Acute Hepatitis
