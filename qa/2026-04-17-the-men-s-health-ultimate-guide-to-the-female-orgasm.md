But that’s not the only thing that happens during arousal. The vagina also lubricates, the vulva becomes more sensitive to touch, and the vaginal canal lengthens and stretches in preparation for penetration. These are all critical steps on the path to pleasurable sex because if someone isn’t fully aroused before penetration, they’re more likely to experience painful intercourse, Blaylock-Solar says.

“Try to remember the goal of sex is pleasure, and orgasm is one kind of pleasure that is significantly shorter than all the rest of it,” says Shadeen Francis, LMFT, a sex therapist in Philadelphia. That’s why she recommends slowing down.

Data backs this up, too: A 2020 study in the Journal of Sexual Medicine found that vulva-owners took roughly twice as long as penis-owners to reach orgasm during penetrative sex (an average of 12.76 minutes and 14.06 minutes, respectively).

15. Take the pressure off orgasming.

We know—this is a guide all about how to achieve an orgasm. Placing too much emphasis on orgasm can make both partners feel an added pressure to perform, Blaylock-Solar adds. One may feel like they have to orgasm (and if they can’t, fake it), while the other may feel like a failure if they don’t do enough to make their partner orgasm.

Ultimately, “it’s important to understand that orgasms are sudden releases of pressure that cannot always be controlled,” says Blaylock-Solar. That doesn’t mean you should just go through the motions. Instead, Blaylock-Solar encourages couples to prioritize pleasure over performance and keep communicating about what they want from their sexual experiences.
