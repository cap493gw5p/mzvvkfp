Explanation

Feeling anxious from time to time is part of being human. But when worry, restlessness, muscle tension, or racing thoughts start interfering with sleep, work, or relationships, it's worth paying attention. Many people turn to natural remedies first—especially chamomile.

But does chamomile actually calm your nerves? And when should you consider something more?

Let's break it down clearly, using credible medical evidence and practical advice you can trust.

What Is Chamomile?

Chamomile is a daisy-like plant that has been used for centuries as a calming herbal remedy. The two main types used medicinally are:

German chamomile (Matricaria chamomilla)

Roman chamomile (Chamaemelum nobile)

Most research on anxiety focuses on German chamomile.

Chamomile is commonly consumed as:

Tea

Capsules or extracts

[Chamomile Calms](https://copperglow.dns0663.com/blog/4729186849.html)

Liquid tinctures

Essential oils (for aromatherapy—not ingestion)

Its reputation? Gentle relaxation and improved sleep.

How Chamomile May Help Anxiety

Chamomile contains active compounds, especially apigenin, a plant flavonoid. Research suggests apigenin may bind to certain receptors in the brain—particularly GABA receptors—which are involved in calming nervous system activity.

This is important because:

GABA helps slow down brain activity.

Lower GABA activity is linked to anxiety.

Many prescription anti-anxiety medications also target this pathway (though much more powerfully).

What Research Shows

Clinical studies have found:

Chamomile extract may reduce symptoms in people with mild to moderate generalized anxiety disorder (GAD).

Long-term use appears to be safe and well tolerated in most healthy adults.

It may modestly reduce relapse of anxiety symptoms over time.

It's important to be clear:Chamomile is not as strong as prescription medication, but it may help take the edge off mild anxiety.

Medically Approved Next Steps for Anxiety

If chamomile alone isn't enough, here are evidence-based approaches doctors recommend:

1. Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT)

CBT is one of the most effective treatments for anxiety. It helps you:

Identify anxious thought patterns

Replace catastrophic thinking

Build coping tools

Gradually face fears safely

For many people, CBT works as well as medication.

2. Medication (When Appropriate)

Doctors may prescribe:

SSRIs (like sertraline or escitalopram)

SNRIs

Short-term anti-anxiety medications in specific cases

These are typically used for moderate to severe anxiety. They are safe when monitored properly but require medical supervision.
