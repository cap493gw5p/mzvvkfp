How Saxenda Works

Saxenda mimics a natural gut hormone called glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1). By activating GLP-1 receptors, it:

Slows gastric emptying (food stays in your stomach longer)

Signals fullness to the brain

Reduces appetite and calorie intake

These effects contribute to weight loss but can also lead to gastrointestinal (GI) side effects.

Common Gastrointestinal Side Effects

Most GI side effects are mild to moderate and tend to improve over time. They include:

Nausea

Vomiting

Diarrhea

Constipation

Abdominal (stomach) pain

Stomach pain with Saxenda often stems from its core actions:

Food lingers in the stomach longer, stretching the stomach wall and triggering discomfort.

Altered muscle activity can cause cramps or sharp pains.

GLP-1 influences nerves that modulate sensation in the GI tract, making mild stretching or gas feel more painful.

While these effects are expected, persistent or severe pain isn't normal.

When to Consider Switching Medications

Switching away from Saxenda may be appropriate if:

You've titrated slowly but still have moderate to severe pain

GI side effects persist beyond 4–6 weeks

Pain affects daily activities or quality of life

You develop other intolerable side effects (e.g., severe nausea, ongoing vomiting)

Alternative Options

If Saxenda isn't tolerable, your doctor might discuss:

Another GLP-1 agonist (e.g., semaglutide) with a different dosing schedule

Medications in a different class (e.g., bupropion/naltrexone, phentermine/topiramate)

Non-drug approaches: nutritional counseling, behavioral therapy, medical devices

Some symptoms warrant urgent care rather than waiting for your next appointment:

Severe, unrelenting abdominal pain

Blood in vomit or stool

Signs of dehydration (dizziness, dark urine)

Difficulty breathing or chest pain

In these cases, call emergency services or go to the nearest emergency department.
