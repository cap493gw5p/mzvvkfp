What symptoms are suitable for urgent care treatment instead of self-care or ER care?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Urgent care centers treat non-life-threatening conditions such as minor cuts, sprains, mild asthma flare-ups, low-grade fevers, and common infections. In contrast, the emergency room is the right choice for serious symptoms like chest pain, difficulty breathing, severe bleeding, stroke signs, or major trauma. Knowing the difference can save time, money, and potentially your life.

If you're unsure where your symptoms fall on that spectrum, don't guess. Explanation

Urgent care centers are a good choice for treating conditions that are not life-threatening but still need quick attention. These include minor injuries such as sprains, small cuts that might need stitches, mild asthma attacks, or fevers that do not go away with home treatment.

When to Choose Urgent Care

Minor Injuries: Sprains, strains, or small cuts.

Mild Illnesses: Fevers, sore throats, or ear infections.
