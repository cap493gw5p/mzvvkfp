Internal Medicine

Nerve pain in the leg after bypass surgery is a known complication that can occur when the saphenous vein is harvested for the procedure. Because the saphenous nerve runs alongside this vein, it may be stretched, bruised, or damaged during removal, leading to symptoms like numbness, tingling, burning, or sharp pain along the inner leg, calf, or ankle. In most cases, this discomfort improves over weeks or months as the nerve heals, but some patients experience lingering sensations.

Because nerve pain can also signal other issues—such as poor circulation, blood clots, or infection—it's important to understand what may be driving your symptoms. Explanation

After bypass surgery, some people may feel nerve pain in their leg. This can happen because the saphenous vein, which runs along the leg, is often used in the surgery.

Why It Happens:

Saphenous Vein Usage: During coronary artery bypass grafting, the saphenous vein is commonly removed from the leg to be used as a graft. This process can sometimes damage nearby nerves, leading to pain.

Nerve Damage: The surgery can cause a condition called saphenous neuralgia. This is when the nerves around the saphenous vein are affected, causing pain or tingling.

Symptoms:

People might feel tingling, numbness, or sharp pain in the leg.

The pain can be constant or happen only sometimes.
