What Are Skin Tags?

Skin tags (medically called acrochordons) are small, soft, flesh-colored growths that hang off the skin. They are not cancerous and are typically painless.

They most often appear in areas where skin rubs against skin or clothing, including:

Neck

Underarms

Groin

Under the breasts

Eyelids

They are especially common in adults over 40, people with diabetes, and individuals who are overweight. Hormonal changes, such as during pregnancy, can also increase the likelihood of developing them.

Do Skin Tags Need to Be Removed?

From a medical standpoint, most skin tags do not require treatment. However, skin tag removal may be recommended if:

The tag becomes irritated or painful

It bleeds repeatedly

It twists on itself

It interferes with shaving or clothing

You're unsure whether it's truly a skin tag

It's also completely reasonable to want removal for cosmetic reasons. Your comfort and confidence matter.

The Medical Reality of Skin Tag Removal

If you're considering professional skin tag removal, here's what actually happens in a medical setting.

1. Proper Diagnosis Comes First

Before removing anything, a healthcare professional will examine the growth. This step is critical. While most skin tags are benign, some skin cancers or other conditions can look similar.

Warning signs that need medical evaluation include:

Rapid growth

Irregular color (dark brown, black, or multiple colors)

Bleeding without injury

A firm or ulcerated surface

If there's any doubt, your doctor may send the tissue for laboratory testing after removal.

2. Common Medical Removal Methods

Doctors typically use one of the following safe, quick procedures:

🔹 Snip Excision

The tag is cut off with sterile surgical scissors.

Often used for small or thin tags.

Usually quick with minimal discomfort.

May require local anesthesia for larger tags.

🔹 Cryotherapy (Freezing)

Liquid nitrogen is applied to freeze the tag.

The tag falls off within days.

Mild stinging or temporary discoloration may occur.

🔹 Electrocautery

A small electrical current burns off the tag.

Helps reduce bleeding.

Often used for larger tags.

These procedures are typically done in minutes during an office visit.

Is Skin Tag Removal Painful?

Most people describe professional skin tag removal as mildly uncomfortable rather than painful.

Small tags may not require anesthesia.

Larger ones may involve a quick numbing injection.

Some minor tenderness or scabbing is normal afterward.

Healing usually occurs within 7–10 days.

Why You Should Be Cautious with At-Home Removal

There are countless over-the-counter products and online tutorials claiming easy DIY skin tag removal. While some are marketed as "natural" or "safe," there are real risks:

Infection

Excessive bleeding

Scarring

Incomplete removal

Misdiagnosing a serious condition

Tying off a tag with string or cutting it yourself may seem simple, but without sterile tools and proper evaluation, complications are possible.

Will Skin Tags Come Back?

Once removed, that specific skin tag typically does not return.

However, some people are prone to developing new skin tags over time, especially if underlying risk factors remain. These may include:

Insulin resistance or diabetes

Obesity

Hormonal fluctuations

Friction from skin folds

Maintaining a healthy weight and managing blood sugar levels may reduce future occurrences, though there is no guaranteed prevention.
