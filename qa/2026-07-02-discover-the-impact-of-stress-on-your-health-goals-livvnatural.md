Understanding Stress

Before we look at the impact of stress on our lives, we need to understand what stress is. Simply put, it refers to a natural human response to challenges and threats in our lives.

[Impact of Stress](https://collagen-atelier.bbsxyxy.com/blog/1126744476.html)

Stress is the state of mental tension, distress, or concern brought on by a difficult situation. Just as everyone has a unique experience, there are also various types of stress. The three primary types are:

Acute: Your body’s reaction to challenging scenarios—this is a short-term reaction. This could be a looming deadline or narrowly escaping a dangerous experience. People also experience acute stress after a personal achievement or exhilarating encounter.

Episodic: Frequent occurrences of acute stress brought on by repeated high-stress situations. Most commonly, we find that healthcare professionals, journalists, and others in pressure-driven jobs experience these episodes.

While the effects seldom have the chance to return to normal, they often cumulate—leaving people feeling like the crises are never-ending.

Chronic: The result of prolonged exposure to stressors like ongoing conflicts. This feeling of never-ending stress often seems daunting. The only way to overcome or improve this circumstance is to address the root cause of the stress.

During challenging events, the hypothalamus (region at the base of the brain) releases the stress hormones adrenaline and cortisol. This internal alarm system prepares your body for its flight-or-fight response.

This automatic physiological reaction is often the result of a frightening or stressful event. The sympathetic nervous system triggers an acute response, preparing the body to flee or stand and fight.

As there are different reactions to stress, there are also varying causes. To overcome the symptoms experienced, you must identify the cause to resolve it. The most common stress triggers are:

Employment: Whether it be the loss of a job or problems at work, the impact of this stress can be intense. Hopelessness, depression, or feelings of being overwhelmed are common experiences. People who experience employment-driven stress also often suffer from burnout.

Health: While stress can cause poor physical and emotional health, it can also result from poor health.These two aspects impact each other, resulting in a vicious cycle.

Death: Losing a loved one can have devastating emotional effects. Not only does this grief take an emotional toll, but it also causes stress.

The Impact of Stress on Your Health

Can stress be positive? Yes. When briefly experienced, stress can be the motivational drive to push for better results.

In the long-term, however, chronic stress can have negative implications on your physical and mental health. Let’s explore the impact of stress when working on your health goals.

Physical Consequences

Though a normal part of life, stress often triggers your body’s natural response to protect from unpleasant situations. When consistent, however, these responses can also be harmful.

The impact of stress over prolonged periods results in high blood pressure and heart rate. Ultimately, this makes the heart work harder and decreases cardiovascular health.

If left unmanaged, chronic inflammation could prohibit the body’s ability to fight off diseases. Along with a vulnerability to illnesses, you’ll also experience slower healing when sick or injured.

Strategies to Manage and Reduce Stress

With the daily grind being what it is, experiencing the impact of stress in your life is inevitable. You can, however, change how you handle stressors. Reaching your health goals comes down to establishing good habits built on good foundations.

Let’s check out some things you can do to build those healthy habits.

Identify symptoms and triggers

Keep an eye out for the common symptoms listed above. If you experience any of them, it’s time to identify the cause.

While it’s simple enough to treat the symptoms, it doesn’t make much of a difference without addressing the cause. Explore recent events to identify what is causing your stress. Next, it’s time to start working on finding a way to deal with it.

Lifestyle changes

Once you know the root of your stress, it’s time to work on laying that firm foundation. Implementing a few healthy habits is the first step to finding peace. Here are some common building blocks to build with:

Regular physical activity helps lower blood pressure, which, in turn, reduces stress hormone levels. Try aerobic activities like walking and dancing to raise your heart rate and get more oxygen throughout your body. Ultimately, increased breathing and more oxygen helps to reduce tension and improve cardiovascular health.

Eating a healthy, balanced diet helps to support your immune system and repair the damaged cells in your body. Vegetables and foods rich in omega-3 fats contain polyunsaturated fats that regulate cortisol levels in the body. Taking the time to practice meal planning helps to encourage mindful, healthy eating.

Deep breathing and practicing meditation helps to reduce muscular tension and lower blood pressure. Continuous stress can often result in erratic thoughts. By calming the mind, you gain better control over your thoughts and gain a better perspective of the situation.

Taking the time for self-care and doing the things you enjoy are effective stress relievers. Aim to take at least an hour a week for yourself.

Find a work-life balance by taking the vacation and personal time available to you. A break now and then allows you to escape the pressures of your everyday life, reducing stress. Taking time off work sounds like a contradiction, but a brief rest helps to boost productivity and reduce burnout.

Set boundaries and don’t be afraid to say no when necessary. You must put your mental health first and not be concerned with prioritizing the concerns of others. Know your limits and don’t allow anyone to disregard them.

Seek help, whether it be social or professional. Once you’ve identified the source of your stress, it’s perfectly acceptable to turn to those around you for help. A friend or family member could be a great sounding board, a counselor could help you develop a much needed angle of approach.

Practice better sleep hygiene to get uninterrupted rest. Better sleep helps to support your immune system and improve mood regulation. Spend at least 30 minutes before bed each night to improve sleep quality and reduce stress. Aim for at least 7–9 hours of uninterrupted sleep each night.
