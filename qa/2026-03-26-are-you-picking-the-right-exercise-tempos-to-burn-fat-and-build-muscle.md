# Are You Picking the Right Exercise Tempos to Burn Fat and Build Muscle?

Most gym goers have one exercise speed: FAST!

While performing explosive reps has benefits, doing every exercise as fast as possible may lead to decreasing stability in your muscle and joints, and deteriorating form and technique.

As a result, your progress will slow, and your risk of injury will increase.

If you’re not varying your exercise tempo—or how fast or how slow you lift a weight—during a workout, you’re missing out. That’s because even the smallest tempo tweaks can enhance fat loss, muscle gain, and performance, and bulletproof your joints.

In order to effectively change up the speed during an exercise, you need to know the four phases of a lift:

Eccentric: This is known as the “negative phase,” and it’s usually the lowering portion of an exercise. It’s also the amount of time your muscles lengthen under load.

Isometric: This is the amount of time you pause at the midpoint of an exercise.

For example, the isometric phase of a squat or pushup would be the bottom before you push back up to the starting position. Or the top of a pullup before you straighten out your arms to go back down.

Concentric: This is known as the “positive phase,” and it’s typically the lifting portion of the move. It’s also the amount of time your muscles shorten under the load.

Between Reps: This is the amount of time you take between each rep of an exercise.

Conventional wisdom tells you to take longer to lower the weight than to lift it, and to take a brief pause at the midpoint.

The tempo would be written out like this: 2-1-1-0.

Translation: You take 2 seconds to lower the weight, pause for 1 second at the midpoint, and take 1 second to lift. Then immediately go into your next rep (0 seconds rest).

This is a very sound and safe training tempo, but there’s one big problem: Your body quickly adapts to this speed. If you want to prevent plateaus and keep your #gainz coming, then you need to mix up your training tempos.

In addition, tempo is especially important if you exercise at home or have minimal equipment. Changing up the tempo can make the same movement and training load feel much more challenging.

CYCLE 1: eccentric/slow lower (3-1-10) You’ll take 3 seconds to lower the weight, pause for 1 second at the midpoint, and take 1 second to lift the weight. There’s no pause between reps (0 seconds).

Studies show that this tempo enhances muscle and strength gains, elevates metabolism, and strengthens your joints and connective tissues.

It also eliminates the stretch reflex or the slingshot-like quality of your connective tissues. This means you won’t be able to bounce up and down like usual and this makes your muscles work so much harder.

CYCLE 2: isometric/midpoint pause (1-3-1-0) You’ll take 1 second to lower the weight, pause for 3 seconds at the midpoint, and take 1 second to lift the weight. There’s no pause between reps (0 seconds).

I like to say that if you want to master any exercise, you have to own the midpoint of the movement. After all, it is the all-important transitional phase of an exercise. If you lack strength, stability, or technique in this phase, the whole rep is compromised.

This type of isometric work will build strength at sticking points or hardest point of the movement (for instance, the bottom of a squat or pushup). It’s also easy on the joints, and ultimately sets the tone for using heavier loads for more reps over time.

CYCLE 3: concentric/slow lift (1-1-3-0) You’ll take 1 second to lower the weight, pause for 1 second at the midpoint, and take 3 seconds to lift the weight. There’s no pause between reps (0 seconds).

Your body is already much stronger in the eccentric phase than in the concentric phase. So slowing down this portion of the movement is pretty much a technical knockout to your muscles. Expect this tempo to make any exercise feel three times harder than normal and prepare for a serious burn.

CYCLE 4: continuous/no pause (2-0-2-0) You’ll go up for 2 seconds and down for 2 seconds without stopping at any point for the entire work period.

This provides a “constant tension” stimulus, a technique that bodybuilders have used for decades to maximize muscle growth and sharpen the mind-muscle connection.

This also deprives your muscles of oxygen and pumps them with blood, creating a maniacal metabolic stress that will make you change.
