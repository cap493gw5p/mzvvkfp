Do Women Need Testosterone? Signs of Deficiency and Your Next Steps

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Yes, women need testosterone, and low levels can cause noticeable symptoms including reduced libido and arousal, low energy, flat mood, weaker muscles and bones, and brain fog. Common causes include aging, menopause, certain medications, and adrenal or pituitary conditions.

If you're experiencing persistent low sex drive, fatigue, or mood changes, a clinician evaluation is the recommended next step. This typically includes a symptom review plus morning blood tests to identify underlying causes. Treatment may focus on lifestyle changes, addressing root causes, or in select cases, carefully dosed and monitored testosterone therapy for hypoactive sexual desire disorder. Because safety red flags and individual factors can change your treatment plan, personalized guidance is essential.

When most people think about testosterone, they think about men. But women need testosterone too. In fact, this hormone plays an important role in a woman's energy, mood, muscle strength, and sexual health.

If you've been feeling "off" and can't quite explain why, low testosterone could be part of the picture. Understanding low testosterone in women symptoms libido changes, and what to do next can help you take smart, informed steps without unnecessary fear.

Do Women Really Need Testosterone?

Yes.

Women produce testosterone in smaller amounts than men. It's made primarily in the ovaries and adrenal glands. Even though levels are lower, testosterone still plays a key role in:

Sexual desire (libido)

Sexual arousal and satisfaction

What Causes Low Testosterone in Women?

Testosterone naturally declines with age. Levels typically peak in a woman's 20s and gradually decrease over time.

Other causes can include:

Menopause or perimenopause

Surgical removal of the ovaries

Certain medications (like oral contraceptives or corticosteroids)

Adrenal gland disorders

Chronic stress

Pituitary gland disorders

Eating disorders or extreme weight loss

Chronic illness

Low Testosterone in Women: Symptoms to Watch For

Low testosterone in women symptoms libido changes are often the first thing people notice — but they are not the only signs.

Here are the most common symptoms:

1. Low Libido (Reduced Sex Drive)

One of the most recognized symptoms is a noticeable drop in sexual desire. This may include:

Reduced interest in sex

Fewer sexual thoughts or fantasies

Decreased responsiveness to sexual stimulation

Difficulty becoming aroused

If libido changes are sudden, persistent, and causing distress, it's worth exploring.

2. Fatigue and Low Energy

Women with low testosterone often describe:

Constant tiredness

Reduced stamina

Feeling "drained" even after rest

Difficulty keeping up with daily tasks

This fatigue can overlap with many other conditions, so it's important not to self-diagnose.

3. Mood Changes

Hormones strongly affect mood. Low testosterone may contribute to:

Irritability

Mild depression

Reduced motivation

Feeling emotionally "flat"

4. Decreased Muscle Mass and Strength

Testosterone supports muscle maintenance. When levels drop, you might notice:

Loss of muscle tone

Increased body fat

Difficulty building strength even with exercise

5. Bone Density Loss

Long-term deficiency may contribute to weakened bones. This becomes more important after menopause when fracture risk increases.

6. Cognitive Changes

Brain fog

How Is Low Testosterone Diagnosed?

Diagnosing low testosterone in women is not always straightforward.

Unlike men, there is no single universally accepted "cutoff" level. Doctors typically look at:

Symptoms (especially low libido and distress)

Blood test results

Overall health history

Blood testing is usually done in the morning when levels are highest. However, testosterone levels in women are much lower than in men, and lab testing can sometimes be imprecise.

Is Low Testosterone Dangerous?

Low testosterone in women is generally not life-threatening by itself. However, it can significantly affect quality of life.

In some cases, symptoms may signal:

Adrenal disease

Severe hormonal imbalances

Underlying chronic illness

If symptoms are severe, sudden, or accompanied by:

Unexplained weight loss

Severe depression

Fainting

Vision changes

Severe headaches

You should speak to a doctor promptly to rule out serious conditions.

Treatment Options

Treatment depends on the cause, severity of symptoms, and your overall health.

1. Addressing Underlying Causes

If another condition is responsible (thyroid disorder, adrenal issue, medication side effect), treating that condition may restore balance.

2. Lifestyle Changes

For mild symptoms, these changes may help:

Strength training to support muscle and hormone balance

Adequate protein intake

Stress reduction

Better sleep hygiene

Limiting excessive alcohol intake

Lifestyle changes alone may not fully correct hormone deficiency, but they can improve overall well-being.

3. Testosterone Therapy

In carefully selected women — especially those with distressing low libido after menopause — low-dose testosterone therapy may be considered.

Medical organizations generally agree that testosterone therapy can help some women with hypoactive sexual desire disorder (HSDD).

However, it should:

Be prescribed by a qualified healthcare provider

Be monitored regularly with blood tests

Use appropriate female dosing (much lower than male doses)

Possible side effects may include:

Acne

Increased body hair

Voice deepening (rare at low doses but potentially permanent)

When Should You See a Doctor?

You should speak to a healthcare professional if:

Low libido is persistent and distressing

Fatigue interferes with daily life

You notice unexplained mood changes

You have symptoms along with menstrual changes

You suspect a hormonal imbalance

If symptoms could be serious or life-threatening — such as severe depression, chest pain, sudden neurological symptoms, or fainting — seek immediate medical care.

Even if symptoms seem mild, getting professional guidance is always the safest approach.
